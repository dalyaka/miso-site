Privacy Policy

Effective July 28, 2026 · applies to Miso for iPhone and Apple Watch

The short version: Miso has no accounts, no servers, no analytics and no ads. Your health data is read from Apple Health with your permission, processed on your iPhone and Apple Watch, and never leaves your devices. We could not look at your data even if we wanted to, because it is never sent to us.

1.Who we are and what this covers

Miso is an independent wellness app for iPhone and Apple Watch, published by an individual developer ("we"). This policy covers the Miso app, its widgets and complications, its watch app, and this website. It explains what the app touches on your device, and, just as importantly, what it does not.

Miso is designed local-first. There is no Miso backend. This policy is therefore mostly a list of things that never happen.

2.What Miso reads from Apple Health

With your explicit permission, granted through the standard Apple Health sheet, Miso reads the following categories. You choose what to allow, and the app works with whatever you grant.

DataWhy Miso needs it
Sleep, including stagesThe night behind her mood, your sleep score and sleep need

You can change or revoke any of these permissions at any time in the Health app: Profile → Privacy → Apps → Miso. Miso keeps working with reduced data, it just knows less about you.

3.What Miso writes to Apple Health

Exactly one thing: when you finish a breathing session with Miso, the app saves those minutes to Apple Health as a mindful session. Nothing else is ever written. If you erase Miso's data, those mindful sessions stay in Apple Health, where they belong to you and can be deleted from the Health app directly.

4.What you enter yourself

Miso may ask you for a few things directly, all optional:

  • Journal tags and notes. Each morning you can tag yesterday (for example caffeine, stress, illness, alcohol, cycle) and add a short note. Some of these tags are sensitive by nature. They are stored only on your device, are used only to find your own patterns, never appear on share cards, and are never transmitted anywhere.
  • Birthday and gender. Used on device to compute heart rate zones and to tailor the journal. Both are optional and editable in Settings.
  • Preferences. Your pet's name, bedtime, workout goal, language, theme and notification choices.

5.Where your data lives

Everything Miso computes (daily scores, baselines, insights, journal entries, the pet's state) is stored in the app's private container on your iPhone, protected by iOS sandboxing and your device encryption.

A small daily summary is shared with Miso's own widgets and with your paired Apple Watch, so they can show the same picture. That transfer uses Apple's frameworks (App Groups and Watch Connectivity) strictly between your own devices. No server is involved, because there is no server.

Data stays as long as you keep it. There is no retention on our side, because there is no "our side": you hold the only copy.

6.What Miso does not do

  • No accounts and no sign-in of any kind
  • No cloud storage and no backend
  • No analytics, telemetry or third-party SDKs
  • No advertising, no tracking, no fingerprinting
  • No selling, renting or sharing of data with anyone
  • No network connections from the app at all
  • Health data is never used for marketing, and never will be

Crash diagnostics, if iOS records any, are stored as local files on your device. They are shared with us only if you personally choose Settings → Data & privacy → Export diagnostics and send them to us yourself.

7.Subscriptions and payments

Miso Premium is an auto-renewable subscription handled entirely by Apple through your App Store account. Payment details never touch the app: we do not see your card, your name or your billing address. Purchase validation happens on device through Apple's StoreKit. What Apple processes is covered by Apple's privacy policy.

8.Your controls

  • Export. Settings → Data & privacy → Export my data gives you a complete JSON copy of everything Miso has computed, up to two years of history.
  • Erase. Settings → Data & privacy → Erase everything deletes Miso's data from your iPhone, its widgets and your paired watch: history, journal, baselines, insights, the widget snapshot and cached pet artwork.
  • Revoke access. Health permissions can be withdrawn any time in the Health app without deleting anything.
  • Notifications. Every notification type has its own switch in Settings, including the "miss you" hello.

Deleting the app removes its container. Combined with the erase step above, no trace of your data remains.

9.Security

Miso relies on the strongest protection available to an iOS app: your data never leaves the device, so there is no transmission to intercept and no server to breach. On device, data sits in the app's sandboxed container, covered by iOS file encryption tied to your passcode and biometrics.

10.Your legal rights (GDPR, CCPA and friends)

Privacy laws like the EU GDPR and the California CCPA give you rights over personal data that companies hold about you: access, portability, correction, deletion, objection. Miso's answer to all of them is the same and it is structural: we hold nothing.

  • Access and portability: use Export my data, it is the complete set.
  • Deletion: use Erase everything and delete the app.
  • Sale or sharing of personal information: none, ever. There is nothing to opt out of.

Health and cycle-related data is "special category" data under GDPR. Miso processes it only on your device, only with your Apple Health consent, and only to show you your own patterns.

11.Children

Miso is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from anyone, children included. All processing stays on the device. Journal topics such as alcohol appear only for users whose self-reported age is 18 or over.

12.Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be published at this address with a new effective date, and material changes will be noted in the app's release notes. Because the app has no server, no change can retroactively affect data we hold, since we never hold any.
